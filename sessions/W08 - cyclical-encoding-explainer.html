The Insight

Time is not a line. Time is a circle.

After hour 23, it doesn't go to hour 24. It wraps back to hour 0. Just like a clock.

✅ How time actually works
So the question becomes: how do we tell the model that time wraps around?
Answer: put each hour on the circle using its x,y coordinates. That's sin and cos.
The Solution

Put every hour on the clock face. Read its x,y position.

Imagine the 24 hours placed on a circle. Each hour has two coordinates — how far right (cos) and how far up (sin). These two numbers together tell the model where on the circle this hour lives.
FORMULA:
hour_sin = sin(2π × hour / 24)
hour_cos = cos(2π × hour / 24)
Why two numbers? Because sin alone can't tell 6 AM from 6 PM — both give sin = ±1.0. You need both x and y to have a unique point.
Everyday analogy: Telling someone a shop is "2 km away" isn't enough. You need "2 km north, 1 km east" — two numbers for a position on a circle.

Why Both?

Sin alone can't tell 6 AM from 6 PM.

❌ Sin Only
sin(6 AM) = 1.0
sin(6 PM) = −1.0
Looks fine? But sin(3 AM) = sin(9 AM) = 0.707. The model sees 3 AM and 9 AM as identical. That's a 6-hour gap!
✅ Sin + Cos Together
3 AM = (sin: 0.707, cos: 0.707)
9 AM = (sin: 0.707, cos: −0.707)
Now they're different! Every hour gets a unique point on the circle. No ambiguity.
One number (sin) gives you the wave. Two numbers (sin + cos) give you the circle. You need the circle.
Beyond Hours

This trick works for anything that wraps around.

🕐
Hour of Day
Period = 24. Late-night listeners wrap around midnight.
sin(2π × h/24)
📅
Day of Week
Period = 7. Sunday and Monday are neighbors, not 6 apart.
sin(2π × d/7)
🗓️
Month of Year
Period = 12. December and January are neighbors. Seasonality wraps.
sin(2π × m/12)
🧭
Wind Direction
Period = 360°. Wind at 359° and 1° blow almost the same way.
sin(2π × deg/360)
🎵
Musical Key
Period = 12 semitones. C and B are one semitone apart on the circle of fifths.
sin(2π × key/12)
📊
The Formula
For anything with period P:
x_sin = sin(2π × value / P)
x_cos = cos(2π × value / P)
The rule: if it wraps around, encode it on a circle. One column becomes two — but the model finally understands that the end connects to the beginning.
Why This Matters at Saavn

Without cyclical encoding, your model has a blind spot around midnight.

❌ Raw hour = 0, 1, 2, ... 23
Model thinks a user at 11 PM is maximally different from a user at midnight. So it builds completely different recommendation strategies for them.
Result: The lo-fi chill playlist that works perfectly at 11:30 PM suddenly stops being recommended at 12:01 AM. The user skips. Engagement drops. Revenue falls.
✅ sin(hour), cos(hour)
Model sees 11 PM and midnight as 0.26 units apart — nearly identical. It builds one smooth recommendation strategy that transitions naturally through the night.
Result: The lo-fi playlist flows seamlessly from 11 PM to 1 AM. User stays in the zone. Session length increases. Churn decreases.
The one-liner:
Don't tell the model what time it is. Tell it where on the clock it is.
